05 — Make it easier to stop

Shorter showers
need a trigger.

“Shower less” is vague. A specific cue is easier to act on.

01

Pick a shorter playlist

Instead of letting music run endlessly, choose 2–3 songs and make the last song your cue to finish.

Example: save 2 min
02

Use one song as a timer

Start one familiar track when you step in. When it ends, rinse and get out.

Simple behavioural cue
03

Move shampoo time off-water

Turn the water off while shampooing or soaping, then switch it back on only to rinse.

Example: save 3 min of flow
If your shower is 2 minutes shorter 16 L saved per shower*
If you shower 6× per week 4992 L saved per year*

*Example calculation based on 8 L/min. Keep the interaction, but replace the flow rate with the value you decide to use from your final source.

06 — A day becomes a year

Small routines.
Huge scale.

118 L/day 43,070 L per person, per year

This is why visualising scale matters. The litres were always there — we just rarely see them all at once.

07 — The target

118 → 100.

The Dutch goal is to reduce average drinking-water use to 100 litres per person per day by 2035.

08 — Make the invisible visible

Water is easy
to ignore
when you can’t see it.

The goal is not to shame people. It is to make everyday water use understandable enough to notice — and simple enough to act on.
